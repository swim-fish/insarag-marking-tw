class="clean-btn tocCollapsibleButton_TO0P">On this page</button></div><div class="theme-doc-markdown markdown"><header><h1>Worksite Triage</h1></header><p>The objective of <a class="term" data-tooltip="Assessment, Search and Rescue Level 2" href="/operations/operational-procedures/asr-levels#asr-level-2-worksite-triage-assessment">ASR2</a> is to assess collapsed structures and identify viable
sites for live rescues. The <a class="term" data-tooltip="USAR Coordination Cell" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/ucc">UCC</a> will use this information to list sites in order of priority
and decide which teams to assign to which sites. One of the considerations for the prioritisation
of worksites is the triage category.</p>
<p>The objective of a triage process is to evaluate triage factors to compare collapsed structures
and decide the order of priority. The key to triage is consistency in the comparison of triage factors.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Note: For Worksite Triage Marking go to <a class="" href="/operations/operational-procedures/marking-and-signalling">INSARAG Marking and Signalling System</a></p>
</blockquote>
<h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="first-order-of-priority-triage-categories-with-victim-information">First order of priority: Triage Categories with Victim Information<a href="#first-order-of-priority-triage-categories-with-victim-information" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to First order of priority: Triage Categories with Victim Information" title="Direct link to First order of priority: Triage Categories with Victim Information" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<p>The level of prioritisation of worksites is based on victim information: number of confirmed alive
victims, number of possibilities of live victims, and if there are only dead victims in the
structure. All worksites with confirmed live victims are complete before the structures with
possible live victims. The worksites with the greatest number of victims are the highest priority.
Buildings with dead only may be assigned to <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> Teams as part of <a class="term" data-tooltip="Assessment, Search and Rescue Level 5" href="/operations/operational-procedures/asr-levels#asr-level-5-total-coverage-search-and-recovery">ASR5</a>.</p>
<p>In order to assist deciding which team goes to which site, the triage teams are requested to
estimate how long the operations will take. The duration can only be estimated if the assessors
have an idea of where the victim is located. Duration will depend on the structure, e.g., building
material and size, and on equipment and expertise. The estimate should be based on general
capability of a team and will always be a rough estimate. Duration estimates will allow the <a class="term" data-tooltip="USAR Coordination Cell" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/ucc">UCC</a> to
assign the larger teams to the move difficult or larger sites that take longer to complete. The <a class="term" data-tooltip="USAR Coordination Cell" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/ucc">UCC</a>
collects information on all confirmed and possible live victims. It does not collect victims on all
deceased, only information that is deemed relevant.</p>
<p>The above triage strategy leads to following four triage categories:</p>
<div class="tableWrapper"><table class="usarTable"><thead><tr><th>Triage Categories</th><th>Victim Information</th><th>Expected Duration of Operation</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td rowspan="1" class="componentCell">A</td><td>Confirmed live victims</td><td>Less than 12 hours</td></tr><tr><td rowspan="1" class="componentCell">B</td><td>Confirmed live victims</td><td>Longer than 12 hours</td></tr><tr><td rowspan="1" class="componentCell">C</td><td>Possible live victims</td><td>Not assessed</td></tr><tr><td rowspan="1" class="componentCell">D</td><td>Deceased only</td><td>Not assessed</td></tr></tbody></table></div>
<ul>
<li class=""><strong>Confirmed live victims:</strong> Means that the <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> assessment team knows that there are people alive
in the collapsed structure.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Possible live victims:</strong> Means that there is a possibility that people are alive in the structure,
but the assessment team cannot confirm whether people are alive or even in the structure. Examples
of possible live victims is when by-standers report missing people, or a collapse of a school that
was in session.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Deceased only:</strong> Means that there are not live victims, but the LEMA may want to send teams to
the site to recover the bodies.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="second-order-of-priority-building-information-and-operational-constraints">Second order of priority: Building Information and Operational Constraints<a href="#second-order-of-priority-building-information-and-operational-constraints" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Second order of priority: Building Information and Operational Constraints" title="Direct link to Second order of priority: Building Information and Operational Constraints" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<p>In cases where the <a class="term" data-tooltip="USAR Coordination Cell" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/ucc">UCC</a> needs to use additional information to list the worksites in order of
priority, building related and operations related information can be used. Examples of useful
information is listed below. This are not placed in the triage categories to avoid the triage
categories becoming complicated.</p>
<p>Building related information includes:</p>
<ul>
<li class=""><strong>Use:</strong> For example, a home, office, school, hospital, etc. will provide an indication of
possible trapped victims.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Size of site (footprint and number of floors):</strong> The larger the building, the longer the
operation will take.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Type of construction:</strong> The heavier the construction material, the longer the operation will take.</li>
<li class="">Building Collapse Category:<!-- -->
<ul>
<li class=""><strong>Inclined:</strong> One, some, or all of the columns and walls have collapsed in a way that floors
collapse on an incline.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Overturn:</strong> Part or all of the building has fallen to the side.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Pancake:</strong> One, some, or all floors have collapsed completely.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Debris heap:</strong> One, some or all of the floors, columns and walls have collapsed resulting
in a heap of debris.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Overhang:</strong> Lower parts of the building have collapsed, leaving the higher part of the building
to hang over the lower part.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li class="">Void space information may also be an issue, based on information from building collapse categories:<!-- -->
<ul>
<li class=""><strong>Big void:</strong> When a space is big enough for a person to crawl. The chances of survival for a
victim are greater in big voids than small voids. “Big” is a relative term, i.e. a big void for a
child will be considerably smaller than a big void for an adult.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Small void:</strong> When a person can hardly move and has to lie more or less still while
waiting for help. In small voids, the chances of injury are higher as people trapped inside have
less space to avoid falling objects and collapsing structural elements.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Factors relating to the operations include:</p>
<ul>
<li class=""><strong>Resource availability:</strong> The more limited resources, the longer the operation will take.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Location of site and teams:</strong> The further from the site the team is, the longer the operation will take.</li>
</ul>
