tocCollapsibleButton_TO0P">On this page</button></div><div class="theme-doc-markdown markdown"><header><h1>Sectorisation, Zones &amp; Worksite</h1></header><h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="sectorisation">Sectorisation<a href="#sectorisation" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Sectorisation" title="Direct link to Sectorisation" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<p>A disaster that warrants international <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> response is inherently a large-scale event. The scale of
destruction may just involve one city, or it may affect a large area involving numerous cities and
even more than one country. Geographical sectorisation of the affected areas can be needed to ensure
effective coordination of <span class="term" data-tooltip="Search and Rescue">SAR</span> efforts by improving the span of control. Sectorisation will improve
operational planning, more effective assignment of the <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> teams and better overall management of
the incident. The size and numbers of the sectors will depend on the level of resources and the
needs of the affected area, the volume of work, geographical area and features, scale of response
amongst other factors.</p>
<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="sectorisation-plan">Sectorisation Plan<a href="#sectorisation-plan" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Sectorisation Plan" title="Direct link to Sectorisation Plan" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>Sectorisation should be undertaken at the earliest possible stage of a disaster response to ensure
its effectiveness. It is expected that the <a class="term" data-tooltip="Local Emergency Management Authority" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/coordination-elements#local-emergency-management-authority-lema">LEMA</a> should have a sectorisation plan in place and that
international USAR teams should follow it. The <a class="term" data-tooltip="Local Emergency Management Authority" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/coordination-elements#local-emergency-management-authority-lema">LEMA</a> is likely to refer to local sectors such as
neighbourhoods, parishes, etc. as the local government will have information organised in this format.</p>
<p>However, if there is no sector plan, it should be developed at the earliest possible stage of a
disaster response and in close liaison with the LEMA. This may be done by the <span class="term" data-tooltip="UN Disaster Assessment and Coordination">UNDAC</span> team but will
often be done by the <span class="term" data-tooltip="On-Site Operations Coordination Centre">OSOCC</span> or <a class="term" data-tooltip="USAR Coordination Cell" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/ucc">UCC</a> personnel from the USAR teams. If the LEMA has no sector plan,
then a wide area assessment (ASR1) may be necessary to get the relevant information to design the
sector plan.</p>
<p>During sectorisation, local geographical and transport challenges should be considered so as to
facilitate the most efficient deployment of resources in the shortest amount of time.</p>
<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="sector-identification">Sector Identification<a href="#sector-identification" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Sector Identification" title="Direct link to Sector Identification" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>The default <span class="term" data-tooltip="International Search and Rescue Advisory Group">INSARAG</span> sector identification system is to use a simple lettering system to code each
sector: A, B, C, D and so on. Do not to use the letter I and O to avoid any misunderstandings with
the numbers 1 and 0. A local name or description can also be added to ensure clarity e.g. Sector A,
North Padang. If the LEMA has its own a coded sector identification system in place (e.g. Sector 1,
2, 3, or Red, Blue, Green etc.), it should be adopted and allow for it in any documentation or markings.</p>

<h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="zones">Zones<a href="#zones" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Zones" title="Direct link to Zones" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<p>Within sectors, zones can be established. A zone is a geographical area (within a sector) attributed
to 1 single <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> team in which that team has the responsibility to identify worksites, prioritise
them and perform USAR operations. Zones are relevant if, during the <a class="term" data-tooltip="Assessment, Search and Rescue Level 2" href="/operations/operational-procedures/asr-levels#asr-level-2-worksite-triage-assessment">ASR2</a> of the sector, the number
of possible worksites is strongly exceeding the operational capacities available. Normally this means
the operational tactics will switch to rapid response mode.</p>
<p>The zone identification does not match the sector it is in. It is made up of the USAR team
identification code, a dash &quot;-”, a single digit followed by the letter “Z”, e.g., USA01-1Z. Zone
identifications are only used on forms and in <a class="term" data-tooltip="INSARAG Coordination and Management System" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/icms">ICMS</a>. No such thing as a zone marking exists.</p>
<h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="worksite">Worksite<a href="#worksite" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Worksite" title="Direct link to Worksite" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<p>To allow effective coordination it is essential to uniquely identify every site where significant
<span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> operations take place. Each of these sites will be known as a worksite.</p>
<p>A worksite can mean different things, but the simplest definition is “Any site where significant
USAR operations are carried out.” <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> operations normally only take place when there is thought to
be the potential for a live rescue. However, to avoid assigning teams to sites that only have victims
to be confirmed dead, these sites may be given an identification (ID) for documentation purposes.
Worksites will typically be one building where one USAR team or squad is working because of a
potential live rescue. But a worksite could be much larger or much smaller. A large building or
complex of buildings, e.g. a hospital, may be identified as a single worksite. Alternatively, the
site of a single rescue in an area of only a few square metres would also be identified as a worksite.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Refer to <a class="" href="/operations/operational-procedures/marking-and-signalling#worksite-triage-marking"><ins>INSARAG Marking and Signalling</ins></a> for Worksite Markings.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="worksite-identification">Worksite Identification<a href="#worksite-identification" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Worksite Identification" title="Direct link to Worksite Identification" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>When it is decided that a site will need USAR operations, usually rescue work, it should be given its
own Worksite Identification (Worksite ID), the Worksite ID is in addition to the existing street name
and building number. Teams allocate a Worksite ID through the <a class="term" data-tooltip="INSARAG Coordination and Management System" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/icms">ICMS</a> tool. In any case, each site should
be allocated its own Worksite ID using the following protocol:</p>
<ul>
<li class="">The first part is the INSARAG team ID e.g., USA02, NED01</li>
<li class="">The second part is a dash “-”</li>
<li class="">The third part is a number between 0001 and 9999.</li>
</ul>
<p>The INSARAG team ID and allocated number produces the unique Worksite ID (e.g. USA02-0056, NED01-1981,
etc.). Teams are to distribute numbering internally between squads so as not to duplicate worksite IDs.</p>
<p>Sector codes will be automatically attached within <a class="term" data-tooltip="INSARAG Coordination and Management System" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/icms">ICMS</a> (e.g., Grant Park-USA02-1981, A-NED01-0056).</p>
<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="usar-team-identification-code">USAR Team Identification Code<a href="#usar-team-identification-code" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to USAR Team Identification Code" title="Direct link to USAR Team Identification Code" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>To standardise the identification of all USAR teams within the coordination system, a team <span class="term" data-tooltip="Identification">ID</span> is
required. The team <span class="term" data-tooltip="Identification">ID</span> is composed of two parts: The three-letter Olympic code of the home country of
the team; and a double-digit number to differentiate teams from the same country. E.g., NZL01, NED01,
FRA03.</p>
<p>Multi-national teams (e.g. <span class="term" data-tooltip="Non-Governmental Organisations">NGOs</span>) will not use the three-letter Olympic code. They will use the
letters “<span class="term" data-tooltip="Search and Rescue">SAR</span>” to be identified. E.g., SAR11, SAR03.</p>
<p>Team designators 01 to 09 are for <span class="term" data-tooltip="INSARAG External Classification/Reclassification">IEC/R</span> classified teams, designators 10 through 99 are to be used
for non-classified teams. The <a class="" href="/fundamentals/membership#insarag-operational-focal-point"><ins>INSARAG Operational Focal Point</ins></a> administers the country team designators
and through the <a class="" href="/fundamentals/governance#insarag-secretariat"><ins>INSARAG Secretariat</ins></a> updates the <a href="https://insarag.org/directory/directory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="">USAR directory</a>.</p></div></article><nav class="docusaurus-mt-lg pagination-nav" aria-label="Docs pages"><a class="pagination-nav__link
