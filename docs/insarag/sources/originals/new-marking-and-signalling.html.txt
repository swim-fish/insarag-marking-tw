page</button></div><div class="theme-doc-markdown markdown"><header><h1>INSARAG Marking and Signalling System</h1></header><h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="insarag-marking-system">INSARAG Marking System<a href="#insarag-marking-system" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to INSARAG Marking System" title="Direct link to INSARAG Marking System" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<p>Marking systems are an essential tool used in <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> operations to display and share key information
between rescue teams and other field personnel. They should also be a mechanism to strengthen
coordination and minimise duplication. To maximise the value of using a marking system in an
event it is necessary to identify and universally use a single, common method. For this method to
be effective, it must be used by all responders, remain simple to apply, simple to understand, be
efficient in the use of resources and time, communicate the information effectively and be
consistently applied.</p>
<p>The <span class="term" data-tooltip="International Search and Rescue Advisory Group">INSARAG</span> Marking System strives to achieve these things and consists of three principle
Marking elements, these being: Triage Marking, Victim Marking and Rapid Clearance Marking (RCM).
These components deliver a comprehensive suite of visual displays that capture critical information
to both inform situational awareness and support planning and coordination.</p>
<p>The <span class="term" data-tooltip="International Search and Rescue Advisory Group">INSARAG</span> Marking System is used by teams as the default marking system in the absence of any
national system in countries where operations are occurring. Marking system use will be determined
by the <a class="term" data-tooltip="Local Emergency Management Authority" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/coordination-elements#local-emergency-management-authority-lema">LEMA</a> in liaison with the <a class="term" data-tooltip="USAR Coordination Cell" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/ucc">UCC</a>.</p>
<p>Countries are encouraged to use the INSARAG Marking system as their national standard which will
assist in times of crisis when international teams are required to be used. The INSARAG Marking
System is designed to complement not compete with national systems.</p>
<p>The following is defined:</p>
<ul>
<li class="">General Area Marking (optional)</li>
<li class="">Structure Orientation (optional)</li>
<li class="">Cordon Markings (optional)</li>
<li class="">Worksite Triage Marking (required)</li>
<li class="">Victim Marking (required)</li>
<li class="">RCM (optional)</li>
</ul>
<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="general-area-marking">General Area Marking<a href="#general-area-marking" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to General Area Marking" title="Direct link to General Area Marking" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>At times some general marking will be required to be applied to assist in navigation and coordination.
This should be limited to essential information only and be as concise as possible.</p>
<ul>
<li class="">General area marking can be applied using spray paint, builders’ crayon, stickers, waterproof
card etc. as determined by the team. The colour should be highly visible and contrasting to the background</li>
<li class="">It may include:<!-- -->
<ul>
<li class="">Address or physical location</li>
<li class="">Landmark or code name (e.g. sugar factory building 1)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li class="">Assigned area or worksites are to be identified individually (see Worksite Marking)</li>
<li class="">If no maps are available, sketch maps are to be produced and submitted to the <a class="term" data-tooltip="USAR Coordination Cell" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/ucc">UCC</a>/<a class="term" data-tooltip="Local Emergency Management Authority" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/coordination-elements#local-emergency-management-authority-lema">LEMA</a>.</li>
<li class="">When producing maps, primary geographical identification should be the existing street name and
building number, when possible. If this is not possible, landmarks should be used as reference and
should be used universally by all actors</li>
</ul>
<div class="image-container"><img src="/img/img-operations/Street-number-identification.png" alt="Street and Number Identification"></div>
<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="structure-orientation">Structure Orientation<a href="#structure-orientation" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Structure Orientation" title="Direct link to Structure Orientation" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>Structure orientation is an optional way of marking. It includes both an exterior and interior
identification:</p>
<ul>
<li class=""><strong>Exterior Identification:</strong> The street address side (front) of the structure shall be defined as “1”.
Other sides of the structure shall be assigned numerically in a clockwise manner from “1”.</li>
<li class=""><strong>Interior Identification:</strong> The interior of the structure will be divided into quadrants. The quadrants
shall be identified alphabetically in a clockwise manner starting at the corner where Side 1 (front)
and 2 meet. Quadrant E (central lobby, elevators, staircases, etc) applies to buildings with multiple
storeys.</li>
</ul>
<div class="image-container"><img src="/img/img-operations/interior-identification.png" alt="Interior Identification"></div>
<p>Multi-storey structures must have each floor clearly identified. If not obvious, the floors should
be numbered as viewed from the exterior. The ground level floor would be designated the “ground
floor” and, moving upward the next floor would be “Floor 1”, etc. Conversely, the first floor below
ground level would be “Basement 1”, the second “Basement 2”, and so on.</p>
<div class="image-container"><img src="/img/img-operations/multi-storey-structure.png" alt="Identifying stories of a multi-storey structure"></div>
<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="cordon-markings">Cordon Markings<a href="#cordon-markings" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Cordon Markings" title="Direct link to Cordon Markings" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>Cordon markings can be used as an option to identify operational work spaces as well as hazardous
areas to restrict access and warn of dangers.</p>

<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="worksite-triage-marking">Worksite Triage Marking<a href="#worksite-triage-marking" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Worksite Triage Marking" title="Direct link to Worksite Triage Marking" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>Worksite triage marking was originally developed to enable teams to communicate to other teams
that they have already worked in a building and thus avoid duplication of effort. The marking
system has since developed as a two-step communication. First of all, a communication for triage
teams to mark structures that they have identified as viable worksites, and secondly, the original
intent of communicating which teams have completed their work in the building.</p>
<p>The much improved <a class="term" data-tooltip="INSARAG Coordination and Management System" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/icms">ICMS</a> system has reduced the need for a building marking system due to better
coordination and communication among teams but is still deemed an essential part of the coordination
system as it displays critical information, is simple to understand and apply, and allows Worksites
to be easily recognised.</p>
<p>The marking should be placed near the point of entry on the exterior of the collapsed structure that
offers the best visibility, in the front, (or as close as possible) or main entry to the worksite.
Whilst key information is required, teams can exercise discretion and adapt to environmental impacts
within these boundaries whilst still maintaining a common, effective and consistent marking system.
The system should complement the <a class="term" data-tooltip="Local Emergency Management Authority" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/coordination-elements#local-emergency-management-authority-lema">LEMA</a>/national systems and can be adapted to work alongside these
as required.</p>
<p>Markings can be made in any colour clearly contrasting to the structures surface, making it highly
visible at any time.</p>
<p>Example of a completed worksite marking system, with all required work completed:</p>
<div class="image-container"><img src="/img/img-operations/worksite-marking-1.png" alt="Example of completed worksite marking system"></div>
<p><strong>Marking Method</strong><br>
<!-- -->Worksite marking should be applied during initial ASR Level 2 Sector Assessment after a site has
been triaged to be a worksite. The marking should be applied to the front, (or as close as possible)
or main entry to the worksite. See <a class="" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/sectorisation-and-zones#worksite"><ins>Worksite</ins></a> at Coordination for more information on Worksites and Worksite ID. The following method should be used when applying worksite marking:</p>
<ul>
<li class="">May draw a directional arrow to confirm exact location of worksite/worksite entry</li>
<li class="">Inside box – displays:<!-- -->
<ul>
<li class=""><a class="" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/sectorisation-and-zones#worksite-identification"><ins>Worksite ID</ins></a> (app. 40cm. font)</li>
<li class=""><a class="" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/sectorisation-and-zones#usar-team-identification-code"><ins>Team ID</ins></a> (app. 10 cm. font)</li>
<li class=""><a class="term" data-tooltip="Assessment, Search and Rescue" href="/operations/operational-procedures/asr-levels">ASR</a> Level completed (app. 10 cm. font)</li>
<li class="">Date</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li class="">Outside of box – displays:<!-- -->
<ul>
<li class="">Any hazard requiring identification e.g. Asbestos (top)</li>
<li class="">Triage category (bottom)</li>
<li class="">Arrow pointed to the exact location of (the entry of) the worksite</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li class="">Updated with <a class="" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/sectorisation-and-zones#usar-team-identification-code"><ins>Team ID</ins></a>, <a class="term" data-tooltip="Assessment, Search and Rescue" href="/operations/operational-procedures/asr-levels">ASR</a> Level completed and date as further levels of work (ASR) are completed</li>
<li class="">Material used can be spray paint, builders’ crayon, stickers, waterproof card etc. as determined
by the team</li>
<li class="">The <a class="" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/sectorisation-and-zones#worksite-identification"><ins>Worksite ID</ins></a> should be approximately 40cm high</li>
<li class="">Draw a box around the painted text (approximately 1.2 to 1.0 metres)</li>
<li class="">After all work on the worksite has been completed and it is determined no further work is
required a horizontal line is to be drawn through the entire worksite marking, below the worksite
ID and above the <a class="term" data-tooltip="Assessment, Search and Rescue" href="/operations/operational-procedures/asr-levels">ASR</a> Levels completed</li>
<li class="">If it is absolutely determined a building only holds deceased the marking is with ASR5 and the
horizontal line as described above only</li>
</ul>
<p>If a team considers there is a need to leave critical additional information at the worksite this
can be added to the worksite marking using plain language in full view when required. This and all
other relevant details should be recorded on the Worksite Triage or Worksite Report forms and
submitted through the <a class="term" data-tooltip="INSARAG Coordination and Management System" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/icms">ICMS</a>.</p>

<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="victim-marking">Victim Marking<a href="#victim-marking" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Victim Marking" title="Direct link to Victim Marking" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>Victim marking is required to identify potential or known casualty (live or dead) locations that
are not obvious to rescuers e.g. below debris/entombed.</p>
<p><strong>Marking Method</strong><br>
<!-- -->The following method should be used when applying victim marking:</p>
<ul>
<li class="">When teams (e.g. Search teams) are not remaining on site to immediately commence operations</li>
<li class="">At incidents involving multiple casualties or where any confusion on exact location from search
operations is possible</li>
<li class="">Markings are done as close as physically possible to the actual surface point identified as the
location of the casualty</li>
<li class="">Material used can be spray paint, builders’ crayon, stickers, waterproof card etc. as determined
by the team</li>
<li class="">The size should approximately 50 cm</li>
<li class="">The colour should be highly visible and contrasting to the background</li>
<li class="">Not intended for use when rescue operations are completed</li>
<li class="">Not to be applied to the front of a structure with the <a class="" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/sectorisation-and-zones#worksite-identification"><ins>Worksite ID</ins></a> unless that is where the
casualties are located</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Progressive Examples</strong></p>

<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="rapid-clearance-marking-system">Rapid Clearance Marking System<a href="#rapid-clearance-marking-system" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Rapid Clearance Marking System" title="Direct link to Rapid Clearance Marking System" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<p>The triage category and marking system is mainly used for potential live rescue sites but can also
include triage category D to allow triage teams to identify buildings that the teams have reason
to believe that there are only deceased remaining in the building and no rescues are possible.
This applies to <a class="term" data-tooltip="Assessment, Search and Rescue Level 2" href="/operations/operational-procedures/asr-levels#asr-level-2-worksite-triage-assessment">ASR2</a>. However, after having completed <a class="term" data-tooltip="Assessment, Search and Rescue Level 5" href="/operations/operational-procedures/asr-levels#asr-level-5-total-coverage-search-and-recovery">ASR5</a> where teams have established there
are no live victims or “Deceased Only” it may be beneficial to mark the site accordingly. Leaving
a recognised “Clear” marking will prevent duplication and have other advantages.</p>
<p>This type of marking is called <strong>Rapid Clearance Marking (RCM)</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Marking Method</strong><br>
<!-- -->The process for applying RCM is as follows:</p>
<ul>
<li class="">A decision must be made by the <a class="term" data-tooltip="Local Emergency Management Authority" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/coordination-elements#local-emergency-management-authority-lema">LEMA</a> to implement this level of marking</li>
<li class="">RCM can only be used when sites can be fully searched quickly or there is strong evidence
confirming no live rescues are possible</li>
<li class="">Two RCM marking options are available, they are: “Clear” and “Deceased Only”</li>
</ul>
<div class="image-container"><img src="/img/img-operations/rapid-clearance-marking-new.png" alt="Rapid Clearance Marking"></div>
<ul>
<li class="">Can be applied to structures that are able to be searched rapidly or where information confirms
there are no live victims or only deceased remain.</li>
<li class="">Can be applied to non-structural areas – cars/objects/outbuildings/debris piles etc. – that
have been searched to standards indicated above.</li>
<li class="">Applied in the most visible/logical position on the object/area to provide the greatest visual impact</li>
<li class="">Diamond shape with a large “C” inside for “Clear,” or with a large “D” inside for “Deceased
Only.” Immediately below, the following is applied:<!-- -->
<ul>
<li class=""><a class="" href="/coordination/international-usar-coordination/sectorisation-and-zones#usar-team-identification-code"><ins>Team ID</ins></a>: _ _ <em>/</em> _ e.g. AUS01</li>
<li class="">Date of Search: _ _/ _ _ _ e.g. 19 Oct</li>
<li class="">Material to be used can be spray paint, builders’ crayon, stickers, waterproof card etc. at
the discretion of the teams.</li>
<li class="">Size: Approximately 20 cm x 20 cm</li>
<li class="">Colour: Bright, contrasting colour to background</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="team-function-identification">Team Function Identification<a href="#team-function-identification" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Team Function Identification" title="Direct link to Team Function Identification" translate="no">​</a></h3>
<ul>
<li class="">Response team identity (country and team name) by uniform, patch, etc.</li>
<li class="">Personnel – the following positions must be colour-coded and labelled in English plain text (vests, arm bands, helmet colour, etc.)<!-- -->
<ul>
<li class="">Management position(s) – white</li>
<li class="">Medical position(s) – red cross/crescent</li>
<li class="">Safety/security position(s) – orange</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li class="">Vehicles must be marked with team name (flag, magnetic sign, etc.)</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="insarag-signalling-system">INSARAG Signalling System<a href="#insarag-signalling-system" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to INSARAG Signalling System" title="Direct link to INSARAG Signalling System" translate="no">​</a></h2>
<p>Effective communications underpin safe field operations, particularly in multi-agency environments.
This is even more critical in international environments where language and cultural differences
also exist. Effective emergency signalling is essential for safe operation at a disaster site.
Having a universally understood emergency signalling system ensures that all personnel operating
on a worksite know how and when to react to signals on the site to ensure safe and effective
operations for rescuers and victims alike. The following steps must be considered:</p>
<ul>
<li class="">All <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> team members should be briefed regarding emergency signals</li>
<li class="">Emergency signals should be universal for all <span class="term" data-tooltip="Urban Search and Rescue">USAR</span> teams</li>
<li class="">When multiple teams are operating on a single worksite, this common understanding should be
reinforced to all personnel involved</li>
<li class="">Signals must be clear and concise</li>
<li class="">Team members are required to immediately respond to all emergency signals</li>
<li class="">Air horns or other appropriate hailing devices should be used to sound the appropriate signals
as follows and located to allow immediate use</li>
</ul>
<h3 class="anchor anchorTargetStickyNavbar_Vzrq" id="evacuate">Evacuate<a href="#evacuate" class="hash-link" aria-label="Direct link to Evacuate" title="Direct link to Evacuate" translate="no">​</a></h3>
